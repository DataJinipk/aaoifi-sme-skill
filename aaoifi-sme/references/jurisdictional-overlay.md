# AAOIFI — Jurisdictional Adoption Overlay

AAOIFI publishes the standards in Bahrain. Whether they have binding force in a given country depends on that country's regulator. This file gives you the adoption posture and the regulatory anchor points by jurisdiction, so when you advise you can name the right authority and the right circular / module / regulation.

> Rule of thumb: when AAOIFI is **fully mandatory**, cite AAOIFI directly. When AAOIFI is **adopted with modification**, cite AAOIFI and then the local instrument. When AAOIFI is **adopted as guidance**, cite the local instrument and reference AAOIFI as principle.

## Adoption tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Countries |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Fully mandatory** | All AAOIFI accounting and Shari'ah standards are binding on IFIs | Bahrain, Oman, Qatar, Sudan, Syria, Jordan, Lebanon |
| **Tier 2 — Mandatory with modification** | Most standards mandatory; some adapted; CSB at regulator level | UAE (HSA framework), Pakistan (SBP-curated Shari'ah Standards list), Nigeria, Yemen, Kuwait (partial) |
| **Tier 3 — Adopted as guidance** | Local standards take precedence; AAOIFI used as reference | Malaysia (MASB FRS Islamic + SAC-BNM), Saudi Arabia (SAMA gradual adoption), Indonesia (DSN-MUI), Brunei, UK (under IFRS) |
| **Tier 4 — Reference only** | No formal adoption; IFIs may follow AAOIFI voluntarily | South Africa, Kenya, Sri Lanka, Türkiye (selective), various emerging markets |

## 1. Pakistan — SBP, SECP, FSC judgment of 2022

Pakistan's IFI landscape is governed jointly by **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** for banking and **SECP** for non-banking Islamic finance (Takaful, modaraba companies, Islamic mutual funds).

### Pakistan's AAOIFI adoption — selective and curated

The SBP has not adopted AAOIFI standards en bloc. Instead, the SBP-appointed **Shariah Advisory Committee** has reviewed AAOIFI standards individually and adopted them via **IBD Circulars**. The current adopted list (verify against latest SBP circular) includes:

- SS 1 (Currencies), SS 3 (Default), SS 5 (Guarantees), SS 8 (Murabaha), SS 9 (Ijarah), SS 10 (Salam), SS 11 (Istisna), SS 12 (Sharikah), SS 13 (Mudaraba), SS 17 (Sukuk), SS 23 (Agency), SS 26 (Takaful), SS 30 (Tawarruq), SS 31 (Gharar), SS 38 (Online financial dealings), SS 40 (Profit distribution), SS 41 (Reinsurance), SS 43 (Insolvency), SS 49 (Wa'd)
- Several others — for the operative list, refer to **SBP IBD Circular Letter No. 03 of 2018** and subsequent updates.

For accounting:
- **FAS 28** (Murabaha), **FAS 30** (ECL), **FAS 32** (Ijarah), **FAS 33** (Sukuk), and **FAS 1 Revised 2021** are adopted via SBP circulars on Islamic banking accounting.
- IFIs prepare AAOIFI-primary financial statements with IFRS reconciliation; the **Companies Act 2017 / Banking Companies Ordinance 1962** require IFRS, but Islamic banks have specific SBP carve-outs.

### Key SBP frameworks for IFIs

- **Shariah Governance Framework (SGF) 2018** — the foundational document. Requires every Islamic bank and conventional bank with an Islamic window to have:
  - A Shariah Board (≥ 3 members) — equivalent to SSB under GSIFI 1
  - A Shariah Compliance Department (Internal Shariah Audit per GSIFI 11)
  - Annual External Shariah Audit (ASIFI 6) by an SBP-approved auditor
  - Annual Shariah Report from the Shariah Board (GSIFI 1)

- **Fit and Proper Criteria for Shariah Board members** — SBP-specific.

- **SBP Prudential Regulations for IFIs** — capital, liquidity, exposure, KYC/AML.

- **IBD Circular Letter 02 of 2020 — Adoption of IFRS 9 / FAS 30**.

### SECP's role

SECP regulates:
- **Modaraba companies** — Modaraba Companies Ordinance 1980; Modaraba Tribunal still functions.
- **Takaful Rules 2012 (Window) and 2005 (Dedicated)** — both reference AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards.
- **Islamic mutual funds** — under the NBFC framework, with SECP-appointed Shari'ah advisors.
- **Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) Shari'ah-compliant screening** — historically via JSE Islamic Index methodology, recently with refinements.

### The Federal Shariat Court 2022 judgment

In April 2022, the **Federal Shariat Court** ruled that **all forms of riba (interest) must be eliminated from Pakistan's economy** within five years (i.e., by 2027). The judgment:
- Reaffirms the binding nature of Shari'ah-compliant finance for Pakistan.
- Pushes the central bank, government, and IFIs to accelerate Shari'ah-compliant alternatives across all financial products.
- Increases the practical relevance of AAOIFI standards for the Pakistani financial system.

### Zakah in Pakistan

The **Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980** mandates Zakah collection from bank accounts on the first day of Ramadan. IFI accounting for Zakah is therefore both a FAS 39 / SS 35 question and a statutory question — see `fas-deep-dive.md` section 8.

## 2. Bahrain — CBB Rulebook Volume 2

Bahrain is the **historical home of AAOIFI** and the regulator with the deepest integration of AAOIFI standards.

- **CBB Rulebook Volume 2** governs Islamic banks. Module **HC (High-Level Controls)** requires AAOIFI Shari'ah governance.
- **CBB AU (Auditors)** requires External Shari'ah Audit per ASIFI 6.
- **CBB PD (Public Disclosure)** requires the SSB Report per GSIFI 1.
- AAOIFI **FAS are the primary accounting basis** — no separate Bahraini FAS exist for IFIs.
- IIRA (International Islamic Rating Agency) provides ratings using GSIFI 10 methodology.

---

## 3. UAE — Higher Shariah Authority (HSA) framework

In 2018 the UAE established the **Higher Shariah Authority (HSA)** at the **Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE)**.

- HSA has **binding fatwa authority** over UAE IFIs — a Tier-2-tilting-Tier-2-strong adoption.
- AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards are the **default reference**; HSA fatwas can vary or supplement.
- Accounting — AAOIFI FAS are required for Islamic banks; conventional banks with Islamic windows follow FAS 40 plus HSA-specific guidance.
- External Shari'ah Audit framework is being rolled out — phased starting 2021.

Key bodies:
- **Securities and Commodities Authority (SCA)** — Shari'ah-compliant equities listing, Sukuk listing on ADX/DFM.
- **Dubai Islamic Economy Development Centre** — promotion; not a regulator.
- **Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA)** — DIFC-based IFIs (e.g., DIFC Islamic Funds). DFSA has its own ISFI module that broadly tracks AAOIFI.

## 4. Saudi Arabia — SAMA gradual adoption

**Saudi Central Bank (SAMA)** has been progressively adopting AAOIFI since around 2017, particularly on accounting. The "Saudi exception" historically:
- Saudi banks historically followed IFRS — even for their large Islamic books.
- Since 2018 SAMA has been steering IFIs towards AAOIFI standards where IFRS does not address Islamic-specific aspects.
- **Saudi Capital Market Authority (CMA)** governs Sukuk listings and Islamic funds.

Saudi Arabia hosts the **Islamic Development Bank (IsDB)** which is itself a major AAOIFI user and contributor.

---

## 5. Qatar — QCB and QFCRA

**Qatar Central Bank (QCB)** is a strong AAOIFI adopter. QCB's Islamic Banking Instructions reference AAOIFI directly.

**Qatar Financial Centre Regulatory Authority (QFCRA)** governs Islamic finance in the QFC — broadly Tier-1 AAOIFI.

---

## 6. Oman — Islamic Banking Regulatory Framework

**Central Bank of Oman's Islamic Banking Regulatory Framework (IBRF) 2012** is one of the most AAOIFI-aligned in the region.
- AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards and FAS apply directly.
- Oman's Higher Shari'ah Supervisory Authority (within CBO) coordinates.

---

## 7. Malaysia — MFRS, IFSA 2013, SAC-BNM

Malaysia operates on a **parallel track** — broadly Tier-3.

- **Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)** governs Islamic banks via the **Islamic Financial Services Act 2013 (IFSA)**.
- The **Shariah Advisory Council of BNM (SAC-BNM)** has **binding fatwa authority** — its rulings supersede individual IFI SSB rulings.
- SAC-BNM's positions differ from AAOIFI on:
  - **Bay' al-Inah** — permitted in Malaysia, prohibited by AAOIFI
  - **Organised Tawarruq** — permitted (with conditions) in Malaysia, prohibited by AAOIFI
  - **Bilateral Wa'd / Muwa'adah** — broader permission in Malaysia
  - **Murabaha Sukuk tradability** — historically more permissive in Malaysia
- Accounting follows **MFRS** (Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards) which are aligned to IFRS, with Malaysian-specific Islamic FS notes.
- AAOIFI is used as **reference** for product structuring but not binding.

Malaysia is also home to **ISRA (International Shari'ah Research Academy)** and **INCEIF (International Centre for Education in Islamic Finance)**.

---

## 8. Indonesia — DSN-MUI fatwas + OJK

- **Otoritas Jasa Keuangan (OJK)** is the integrated financial regulator.
- **Dewan Syariah Nasional - Majelis Ulama Indonesia (DSN-MUI)** issues binding Shari'ah fatwas.
- DSN-MUI fatwas are typically aligned with AAOIFI but with Indonesia-specific adaptations.
- Accounting: **PSAK Syariah** (Indonesian Sharia Accounting Standards) — broadly aligned with AAOIFI but with Indonesian modifications.

---

## 9. UK and other non-OIC markets

- **UK** — IFIs operate under FCA / PRA. No separate Islamic-finance regulator. Accounting under IFRS. AAOIFI used as voluntary reference.
- **South Africa** — SARB regulates; AAOIFI used as reference. Albaraka Bank South Africa is the principal IFI.
- **Türkiye** — Participation Banking framework under BDDK. AAOIFI used as reference; Türkiye has its own Participation Banks Association of Türkiye (TKBB) Shari'ah Board.
- **Sri Lanka** — Amana Bank operates under the conventional CBSL framework; AAOIFI used voluntarily.

## 10. The "selective list" question — which standards each regulator has actually adopted

When a user asks "is FAS X applicable to my IFI in country Y?", the answer depends on the selective adoption list. Approach:

1. **Identify the jurisdiction tier** (using section 1 above).
2. **For Tier 1**, AAOIFI applies fully — answer accordingly.
3. **For Tier 2**, check the **specific regulatory circular** that adopts the standard. In Pakistan, this means SBP IBD circulars; in the UAE, HSA resolutions; in Nigeria, CBN guidelines.
4. **For Tier 3**, check the **local equivalent** — MFRS in Malaysia, PSAK Syariah in Indonesia, SOCPA in Saudi Arabia.
5. **For Tier 4**, AAOIFI is reference-only; default to IFRS plus voluntary Shari'ah governance.

For a multi-jurisdictional IFI (e.g., a UAE bank with a Bahrain subsidiary and a Malaysian Sukuk listing), you may need to give **two or three parallel answers**.

---

> **Closing note.** Adoption status changes. Verify the current circular list before issuing a deliverable. For Pakistan in particular, the SBP IBD Circular Letter series is updated regularly and is the single most-cited source on what is and isn't formally adopted.
